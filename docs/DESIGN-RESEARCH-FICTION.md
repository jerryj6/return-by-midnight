# Design research — the cover of a puzzle game: setup-payoff planting across a 12-case campaign

Source framing: museum-heist fiction (the *Ethereal Return*–adjacent register —
quiet, toy-museum, properties-borrowed-and-returned) and how heist/case-fiction
structures plant and pay off knowledge across a season. Applied to RBM's
RBM-01…12 arc.

## F1. The title is the contract, not the theme [Conv]

"Return by Midnight" names the *mechanic*: everything you borrow must come
home. Heist fiction does the same — the film is named for the rule the crew
lives under (Ocean's *Eleven* counts members; RBM counts returns). Rule:
every level title should whisper its constraint, never its set dressing.
rbm-10 "The Quietest Exit" does this — the level is about not making noise.
Titles that name scenery instead of the constraint mislead the player's
mental ledger.

## F2. Plant the prop before you load it (Chekhov's prop table) [Conv]

Heist fiction plants the laser grid in act one *as environment* and arms it
in act three *as mechanism*. RBM's version: checkable props must appear as
furniture before they're load-bearing (already an authoring rule from the
Papers Please pass — the fiction layer is where it pays off). The
mid-campaign inversion — a lamp that was scenery for two levels suddenly
feeds a gate — is the reveal, and it only lands if the plant was honest:
same id, same cell, visible the whole time.

## F3. The complication is the tutorial (Ocean's beat structure) [Conv]

Each Ocean's complication isn't an obstacle, it's the scene that teaches the
crew's real capability. RBM's level progression already works this way:
rbm-04's "the owner rides while its weight is away" is a complication that
teaches posting-into-future-cells. Campaign rule: the chapter blurb should
name the *complication* ("the owner rides while its weight is away") rather
than the goal — the player should be able to read the arc as a list of
escalating wrenches, not a list of locks.

## F4. Two-ledger storytelling (plan ledger + fate ledger) [Rec]

Heist films intercut the plan (as pitched) with the night (as it happens);
the drama lives in the delta. RBM has the same two ledgers mechanically:
the manifest (what we owe) and the order grid (what we'll do). The
superseded-verdict banner is now the *third* ledger — the record vs the
live draft. Fiction application: a campaign's payoff beats are the levels
where the two ledgers diverge by design — a plan that's legal on paper but
fragile in execution (rbm-05/07's tight density) is the "the plan was
perfect, the night wasn't" moment.

## F5. Season finale = earlier rules composed, not new rules [Conv]

The heist finale works because the audience reassembles earlier tricks.
rbm-11/12 are exactly this: crossover (11) and inverted-home-plate (12) are
compositions of the plate/posting vocabulary taught since rbm-01, not new
verbs. The fiction rule that protects it: never introduce a new mechanic in
the finale's first hour — let the audience feel *smart*, not surprised.
rbm-12's third distinct structure (split postings) is the good kind of
finale surprise: the rules were all visible; the paperwork wasn't.

## F6. The epilogue object (one item that proves the whole night) [Rec]

Every heist story ends on a small object — the canvas that made it out.
RBM's is TOKEN-B/the safe: `tokenHome` predicates make the ending about
*where things rest*, not what was done. Campaign rule: the final verdict
line should always resolve to a *home*, and the epilogue copy
("Everything home by midnight") already does exactly this — keep it uniform
across all 12 closes, including superseded ("Plan moved on without it").

## Applied checklist

- [ ] Every level title names the constraint, not the scenery.
- [ ] New checkable props appear as furniture one level before they're load-bearing.
- [ ] Chapter blurbs name the complication, not the goal.
- [ ] Difficulty peaks where the two ledgers (manifest vs orders) diverge.
- [ ] Finales compose taught vocabulary; never debut mechanics.
- [ ] Every close resolves to a home — verdict and epilogue copy stay uniform.
